    1207 – 1273 · Balkh to Konya

    Maulana Jalāl al-Dīn Rūmī

    Born in Balkh, in the lands of old Khorasan, and carried westward by the coming of the Mongols, Rūmī settled at last in Konya, in the heart of Anatolia. There he grew into a jurist and teacher of standing — until, in his late years, he met the wandering dervish Shams of Tabriz, and everything turned. Their companionship, and its loss, opened in him a fountain of poetry.

    From that fire came the Masnavī-yi Ma‘navī — six books of rhyming couplets, dictated over the last years of his life, that his followers came to call “the Qur’ān in the Persian tongue.” It begins with the lament of the reed cut from its bed, longing to return — an image of the soul parted from its Origin, which is the burden of the whole work.

    After his death, his followers gathered as the Mevlevî order, and remembered him in the turning dance of the samā‘ — one hand raised to receive, one lowered to give, the whole body a reed through which the breath of God might pass.
